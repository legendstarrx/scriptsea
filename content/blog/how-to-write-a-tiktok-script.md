The best TikToks don't sound scripted. That's exactly why most of them are.

When you watch a creator tell a story that flows perfectly, with a strong opening, no rambling and an ending that makes you comment, that didn't happen by accident. They knew the first line, the order of the beats and the last line before they hit record.

Here's how to write a TikTok script that keeps the natural, talking-to-a-friend feel the app rewards, with templates for the most common formats and full examples.

## What makes TikTok scripts different

TikTok is built on the For You feed. Most people who see your video don't follow you yet. They're deciding in the first second or two whether you're worth the next ten.

That shapes three things about how you write:

1. **It has to feel native.** Polished, ad-style delivery stands out in a bad way. Write the way you'd say it to a friend, not the way you'd write it in an email.
2. **It has to work with sound off.** Lots of people scroll muted, so key lines go on screen as text.
3. **It has to give people a reason to react.** Comments, shares and replays help a video travel. A script can invite those on purpose.

## Write the hook for sound on and sound off

Your hook needs two versions: the line you say and the text on screen. They can be the same, or the text can be a short headline version.

> Said: "I quit my job to sell candles, and here's what my first month actually made."
>
> On screen: "First month selling candles 👀"

Keep the spoken hook under about 12 words, and make the first frame match it. If you're talking about a result, show the result. If you're telling a story, start at the most dramatic moment.

## Choose your format first

Most TikToks fit one of a handful of formats. Pick the format before you write, because each one has its own shape.

| Format | Best for | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Storytime | Personal stories, drama, lessons | Tense moment, backstory, twist, ending |
| Tutorial | Tips, how-tos, hacks | Result, steps, final result |
| List | Recommendations, mistakes, facts | Promise a number, deliver each one fast |
| Reply to a comment | Questions from your audience | Read the comment, answer it directly |
| Series | Long stories, ongoing projects | Part number, recap line, new update, cliffhanger |

## Template 1: Storytime (30 to 60 seconds)

> **Hook:** Start at the most tense or surprising moment.
>
> **Backstory:** One or two lines of context. Only what's needed.
>
> **Build:** What happened next, beat by beat. Keep each beat to one sentence.
>
> **Twist or payoff:** The moment the story was building to.
>
> **Ending:** A one-line takeaway, or a question that invites comments.

**Example:**

> "My first client paid me with a cheque that bounced.
>
> I'd just started freelancing, and I was so happy to get a real company I didn't ask many questions.
>
> I did three weeks of work. They loved it. They sent the cheque. I went to the bank and it bounced.
>
> I emailed. Nothing. I called. Disconnected number.
>
> So now I take 50% up front, no exceptions.
>
> Has this happened to you? Tell me I'm not the only one."

That's about 80 words, roughly 30 seconds. Every line moves the story forward, and the last line gives people an easy reason to comment.

## Template 2: Tutorial (15 to 45 seconds)

> **Hook:** Show or say the result first.
>
> **Steps:** Each step is one short line, with the action on screen.
>
> **Result:** Show it again, finished.
>
> **Ending:** One line on when or why to use it.

**Example:**

> "This is the easiest way to get crispy potatoes without a deep fryer.
>
> Boil them for 8 minutes. Drain and shake them in the pot to rough up the edges.
>
> Toss them in oil, salt and a spoon of flour.
>
> Oven at 220°C for 40 minutes, flip once halfway.
>
> Look at that crunch.
>
> Save this for your next roast dinner."

## Template 3: List (20 to 45 seconds)

> **Hook:** Promise a specific number and a clear benefit.
>
> **Items:** One line each, fast. Put the strongest item last.
>
> **Ending:** Point to the best one, or ask which one they'll try.

Number the items on screen as you go. It tells viewers how far through they are, and a lot of people stay just to see the last one.

## Writing longer TikToks

TikTok isn't only short videos anymore. Many creators post videos over a minute, and longer videos matter if you want to earn from the app. TikTok's Creator Rewards Program pays for original videos longer than one minute. To join, TikTok asks for an account in good standing, at least 18 years old, with at least 10,000 followers and 100,000 video views in the last 30 days, in a country where the program is available. Check the requirements in the app before you plan around them, since they can change.

A longer TikTok still needs a short-video opening. The difference is that you have to hold attention for longer, so:

- **Re-hook every 15 to 20 seconds.** A line like "but that's not even the worst part" gives people a reason to keep going.
- **Change the picture often.** New angle, B-roll, a quick cut to what you're describing.
- **Use the series format.** If a story runs long, split it into parts and end each one on a cliffhanger.

At a natural pace, a 60-second TikTok is about 150 words, and a 90-second one about 225.

## Write for comments and shares

A script can make it easy for people to react:

- **End on a real question.** "Which one would you pick?" works better than "Let me know what you think."
- **Leave room for opinions.** A clear stance gives people something to agree or disagree with.
- **Make it saveable.** Tips, lists and recipes get saved. Say "save this" only when the video is actually worth coming back to.
- **Answer comments with videos.** Replying to a comment is one of the easiest video ideas you'll ever have, and the person who asked is likely to share it.

## Make it sound unscripted

The goal is a script that sounds like you're not reading one. A few ways to get there:

- Write in short lines, one thought per line, and say them the way you'd text them.
- Use contractions and the words you'd actually use. If you'd never say "utilize", don't write it.
- Practise it two or three times, then film without reading word for word. Keep the hook and the last line exact, and let the middle be a little loose.
- Cut every line that starts with "so basically" or "I just wanted to say".

## Common TikTok script mistakes

- **A slow open.** "Hey guys, so today I want to talk about..." loses people before the point.
- **No on-screen text.** Muted viewers have no idea what the video is about.
- **Too many ideas.** One story, one tip, one list per video.
- **Sounding like an ad.** Formal phrases and hard selling get swiped fast.
- **A flat ending.** End on the payoff or a question, not "okay bye".

## FAQ

### How long should a TikTok script be?

About 35 words for 15 seconds, 75 to 80 for 30 seconds and around 150 for a minute. If you want to earn through Creator Rewards, your videos need to be longer than one minute.

### Do I need a script for TikTok?

You don't need a word-for-word script, but even a few lines for the hook, the beats and the ending make your videos tighter. Most natural-sounding creators plan more than it looks.

### What should I put as on-screen text?

Your hook as a short headline in the first frame, plus key words or numbers as you go. Keep it big, short and away from the edges where the buttons and caption sit.

### How do I write a TikTok series?

Start each part with "Part 2" (or 3, 4...) and a one-line recap, deliver one new chunk of the story, and end on a cliffhanger. Pin the parts or link them so new viewers can catch up.
