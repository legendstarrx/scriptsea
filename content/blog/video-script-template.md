Staring at a blank page is the hardest part of making a video. You know what the video is about, but not how to start, what goes in the middle or how to end it.

A template fixes that. It gives you the shape of the video, so all you have to do is fill in your words. Below are six free video script templates for the most common kinds of video, each with a short filled-in example. Copy the one you need and change it for your topic.

## How a video script is laid out

Before the templates, a quick word on format. Most video scripts use one of two layouts.

**One column (spoken words only).** Good for talking-head videos, Shorts and Reels, where the picture is mostly you. Write what you'll say, line by line, and add short notes in brackets for anything on screen.

**Two columns (audio and visuals).** Good for anything with b-roll, screen recordings or AI clips. The left side is what we hear. The right side is what we see at that moment.

| Audio | Visual |
| --- | --- |
| "Most people charge their phone wrong." | Close-up of a phone plugged in overnight |
| "Here's what actually happens to the battery." | Simple battery graphic draining |
| "Fix this one setting tonight." | Screen recording of the settings menu |

If you make videos with AI, the two-column layout is the one to use. Each row becomes a scene, and each scene becomes a prompt. We show the full process in [how to make an AI video from a script](/blog/how-to-make-an-ai-video-from-a-script).

## How many words for your video length

At a natural speaking pace, people say about 2.5 words per second, or roughly 130 to 160 words a minute. Use this to size your script:

| Video length | Words |
| --- | --- |
| 15 seconds | about 35 |
| 30 seconds | about 75 |
| 60 seconds | about 150 |
| 3 minutes | about 400 to 480 |
| 8 minutes | about 1,050 to 1,300 |
| 10 minutes | about 1,300 to 1,600 |

Read your script out loud and time it. That's always more accurate than counting.

## Template 1: Short-form video (Shorts, Reels, TikTok)

**Best for:** tips, facts, quick stories and anything under 60 seconds.

```
HOOK (0-3s): The most interesting thing, said first.
SETUP (3-8s): One line of context, only if needed.
VALUE (8-50s): The tip, list or reveal, in short lines.
PAYOFF (50-60s): The best point, a twist, or a line that loops back to the start.
```

**Example (30 seconds):**

> "Your phone has a hidden ruler, and almost nobody knows it's there.
>
> It's in the Measure app, which comes free on most iPhones.
>
> Point the camera at anything, tap once at one end and once at the other. It tells you the length.
>
> On newer Pro models, it can even measure how tall someone is.
>
> I used it to check if a sofa would fit through my door before I bought it. It didn't. Saved me a return."

Short-form lives or dies on the first line. ## Template 2: Tutorial or how-to

**Best for:** teaching a skill, a setup or a fix.

```
HOOK: The result they'll get, or the mistake they're making.
WHY IT MATTERS: One or two lines on why this is worth doing.
WHAT YOU NEED: Tools, apps or ingredients, briefly.
STEPS: Numbered, one action per step, in order.
COMMON MISTAKE: The one thing that trips people up.
RECAP AND NEXT STEP: Quick summary, then what to do or watch next.
```

**Example (opening and first step):**

> "By the end of this video, your laptop will start up in under 20 seconds.
>
> Most slow start-ups aren't the computer's fault. It's apps that open on their own every time you turn it on.
>
> All you need is your laptop and two minutes.
>
> Step one. Open Task Manager. Right-click the taskbar and click Task Manager..."

Keep each step to one action. If a step has "and then" in it, it's probably two steps.

## Template 3: Story video

**Best for:** personal stories, true stories, case studies and storytelling content.

```
HOOK: Start in the middle of the moment, or with the stakes.
CONTEXT: Who, where, and what they wanted.
PROBLEM: What went wrong or stood in the way.
TURNING POINT: The moment things changed.
RESULT: How it ended.
LESSON: One sentence the viewer takes away.
```

**Example (60 seconds, opening):**

> "In 2008, two friends were stuck in a Paris snowstorm, and they couldn't get a taxi.
>
> Their names were Travis Kalanick and Garrett Camp, and they were in town for a tech conference.
>
> Standing in the cold, they asked a simple question: what if you could tap a button and a car just came to you?"

Notice the example starts with a specific moment, not "today I want to tell you about". For more, see our guide on [how to write a storytelling video script](/blog/storytelling-video-script).

## Template 4: Product ad

**Best for:** Facebook, Instagram and TikTok video ads.

```
HOOK (0-3s): The problem or the result, not the brand name.
PROBLEM (3-8s): Make the right viewer think "that's me".
PRODUCT (8-15s): Show it, name it simply.
PROOF (15-25s): Demo, real review, number or before and after.
CALL TO ACTION (25-30s): One clear next step.
```

**Example (30 seconds):**

> "If your phone dies before lunch, this is for you.
>
> Carrying a charger, a cable and a power bank everywhere gets old fast.
>
> This power bank clips straight onto the back of your phone. No cable. It just sticks on and charges.
>
> Watch. Clip it on, and the battery starts going up straight away.
>
> It's on the link below if you want one."

The angle you choose changes the whole ad. ## Template 5: YouTube video (8 to 15 minutes)

**Best for:** long-form YouTube videos, explainers, reviews and list videos.

```
HOOK (0-30s): The promise of the video, shown or said fast.
INTRO (30-60s): Why trust you, and what's coming. Keep it short.
SECTION 1: First point, with an example.
RE-HOOK: A line that pulls them into the next part.
SECTION 2: Second point, with an example.
RE-HOOK
SECTION 3: The strongest point, saved for last.
WRAP-UP: Quick recap, then point to the next video.
```

**Example (opening):**

> "These are the five cheapest places in Europe you can actually live well on $1,500 a month, and number one surprised me.
>
> I spent six months checking rent, food and transport prices in each of these cities, so you don't have to.
>
> Let's start with number five..."

Re-hooks matter most in long videos. A simple "but the next one is even cheaper" every couple of minutes gives people a reason to stay. The full process is in [how to write a YouTube script](/blog/how-to-write-a-youtube-script).

## Template 6: Faceless voiceover video

**Best for:** faceless YouTube channels, documentary-style videos and b-roll explainers.

```
COLD OPEN: A dramatic or surprising line over strong visuals.
TITLE MOMENT: What this video is about, in one line.
CHAPTERS: Each one a mini story or point, with a visual for every line.
REVEAL: The answer, the twist or the biggest fact.
OUTRO: One closing thought, then the next video.
```

**Example (two-column, opening):**

| Audio | Visual |
| --- | --- |
| "In 1872, sailors spotted a ship drifting in the Atlantic." | An old sailing ship drifting on grey waves |
| "Its cargo was still on board. There was food in the stores." | Barrels and crates in a dim ship's hold |
| "But every single person on it was gone." | An empty deck, ropes swinging in the wind |
| "This is the story of the Mary Celeste." | Title over an old sea chart |

For faceless videos, every line needs a visual. Plan them in the right column as you write, not after. If you're starting a faceless channel, read [how to start a faceless YouTube channel with AI](/blog/faceless-youtube-channel-with-ai).

## Tips that work for every template

- **Write how you talk.** Short sentences, everyday words. If you wouldn't say it out loud, rewrite it.
- **One idea per line.** It's easier to read, record and edit.
- **Hook first, always.** No greetings or "in this video" before the point.
- **Read it out loud** before you record. You'll catch every awkward sentence.
- **Cut 10 to 20 percent.** Almost every first draft is too long.
- **End with somewhere to go,** like a next video, a link or a question, not "thanks for watching".

## FAQ

### What is the best format for a video script?

For talking-head videos, a one-column script with just the spoken words works best. For videos with b-roll, screen recordings or AI clips, use two columns: audio on the left and visuals on the right.

### How long should a video script be?

About 2.5 words per second of video. That's roughly 75 words for 30 seconds, 150 for a minute and 1,300 to 1,600 for a 10-minute video. Read it aloud to check.

### Do I need a script for short videos?

Even a 15-second video works better with a script. Writing the hook and the main point first stops you from rambling and makes editing faster.

### Can I use AI to write a video script?

Yes. AI is great for first drafts, hooks and structure. Read the script out loud and change anything that doesn't sound like you before you record.
