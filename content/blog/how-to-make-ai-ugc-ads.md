UGC ads work because they don't look like ads. A person talking to their phone in their kitchen feels like a recommendation from a friend, not a brand trying to sell you something.

The problem is that real UGC is slow and expensive. You find creators, send products, wait for footage and hope it's usable. AI video tools can now make creator-style clips in minutes, which is why so many brands are testing AI UGC.

But most AI UGC ads look fake within two seconds. Plastic skin, perfect lighting, a voice with no energy and a background that looks like a showroom. And some cross a line they shouldn't, like an AI person pretending to be a real customer.

This guide walks through how to make AI UGC ads that look natural, sell well and stay within the rules.

## Step 1: Start with the product page and real reviews

Good UGC ads are built on things that are actually true. Before you write anything, collect:

- **The main problem** the product solves, in the customer's words.
- **The result people mention most** in reviews.
- **The biggest doubt** buyers have, like price, size or "will it work for me".
- **The proof you can show:** a demo, a before and after, a number.
- **The offer:** price, guarantee, bundle or free shipping, only if it's real.

Reviews are where the best lines come from. If lots of customers write "I finally stopped waking up with a sore neck", that phrase belongs in your ad.

## Step 2: Write a script that sounds spoken

UGC scripts should sound like someone talking, not someone reading. Short sentences. Everyday words. One idea per line.

A simple structure for a 30-second UGC ad:

| Part | What it does | Rough time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Stops the scroll | 0 to 3s |
| Problem | Makes the right person think "that's me" | 3 to 8s |
| Product | Shows it and names it simply | 8 to 15s |
| Proof | Demo, result or real review | 15 to 25s |
| Call to action | One clear next step | 25 to 30s |

At a natural pace, 30 seconds is about 70 to 80 words. Our guide on [how to write a UGC ad script](/blog/how-to-write-a-ugc-ad-script) goes deeper, and [7 video ad script examples](/blog/video-ad-script-examples) gives you a script for each common angle.

## Step 3: Keep the AI creator honest

This is the step most guides skip, and it matters.

In the US, the FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials, finalised in 2024, bans reviews and testimonials that pretend to come from someone who doesn't exist, and it names AI-generated fake reviews as an example. It also covers testimonials that misrepresent someone's experience with a product. Other countries have similar consumer protection rules.

So an AI person saying "I've used this every night for a month and my skin cleared up" is a problem. That person never used anything.

You can still use AI creators. Just give them an honest role:

- **The presenter.** The AI creator explains and demonstrates the product, like a host: "Here's why this bottle keeps water cold for 24 hours."
- **The review reader.** The creator shares real customer reviews, clearly as reviews: "This is what people are saying about it."
- **The problem explainer.** The creator talks about the problem in general terms, then shows how the product works.

And when you have real customers, use their real words. That's always the strongest proof. This isn't legal advice, so if you're unsure about a claim, check with someone who knows the rules where you advertise.

## Step 4: Create your creator once

Your AI creator needs to look the same in every clip, so make them once as a reference image and reuse it.

What makes an AI creator look real:

- **Normal, everyday clothes.** A hoodie, a t-shirt, a simple top. Nothing that looks like a photo shoot.
- **Real skin.** Ask for natural skin texture, small marks and slightly uneven tone. Perfectly smooth skin is the biggest giveaway.
- **Natural hair.** A loose strand, a slightly messy bun.
- **A relatable look for your buyer.** The creator should look like someone your customer would trust.
- **One outfit, described the same way every time,** if you describe it at all.

Then attach that image to every clip. A reference image keeps the face far more consistent than any written description. We explain why in [why your AI characters keep changing faces](/blog/ai-character-consistency).

## Step 5: Make a real-looking scene

The background sells the realism as much as the person. Make the location as its own image, with nobody in it, so you can reuse it.

- **Lived-in, not perfect.** A bedroom with a few things on the bedside table, a kitchen with a kettle and a fruit bowl.
- **Natural light.** Daylight from a window, not studio lights.
- **True-to-life colour.** Not too warm and orange, not too cold and blue.
- **No heavy blur.** Real phone footage has most of the room in focus. A creamy blurred background looks like a film, not UGC.

## Step 6: Write the clip prompts

Now turn each part of the script into a clip prompt. For each one, include:

- **The creator and scene**, attached as references.
- **The exact dialogue in quotes.** Enough words to fill the clip, so there are no long silent gaps. Roughly 2 to 3 words per second.
- **Natural behaviour:** natural blinking, small head movements, hand gestures while talking, real energy in the voice. Not a stiff, flat delivery.
- **Camera:** usually static, at eye level, like a phone propped up. You can vary the angle between clips (a close-up, then a side angle) to keep it moving.
- **Sound:** the voice and room tone. Write sound effects for actions, like a cap clicking open.
- **"No subtitles, no captions, no on-screen text"**, because many models add them on their own. You'll add your own captions in the edit.
- **"No music"**, so each clip doesn't come out with a different track.

Here's an example in the presenter style:

> Use the creator and the bedroom scene exactly as they are in the references. She holds up the water bottle, taps the lid and says with real energy, "So this is the bottle everyone keeps asking about. It keeps water cold for a full day, and the lid doesn't leak, watch." She turns it upside down and shakes it. Natural blinking, relaxed hand gestures, a quick smile. Natural daylight from the window. Realistic, unpolished phone video look with natural skin texture. Audio: her voice, the lid clicking, quiet room tone. No music. No subtitles, no captions, no on-screen text.

Fewer, longer clips (around 8 to 10 seconds) usually keep the voice and face more consistent than lots of tiny ones. For model-specific tips, see our guides for [Seedance](/blog/seedance-prompt-guide), [Kling](/blog/kling-ai-prompt-guide) and [Veo](/blog/veo-3-prompt-guide).

## Step 7: Edit like a real UGC ad

The edit is where AI clips become an ad:

- **Trim hard.** Cut dead starts and ends so the talking never pauses.
- **Add captions** in big, clear text. Most people watch with the sound off.
- **Add small sound effects** for actions: a click, a pour, a tap.
- **Use punch-ins in the edit** to add movement, rather than asking the model for zooms.
- **Put the hook on screen** as text in the first frame.

## Step 8: Label it where the platform asks

Platforms are adding rules for realistic AI content:

- **TikTok** asks creators to label AI-generated or heavily edited content that shows realistic scenes or people.
- **YouTube** asks creators to disclose realistic altered or synthetic content that viewers could mistake for real.
- **Meta** adds an "AI info" label to some ads automatically when it detects AI tools, and requires disclosure for political and social issue ads with realistic AI media.

These rules change often, so check each platform's current policy before you launch. Labelling doesn't stop a good ad from working. A misleading ad, on the other hand, can get rejected or damage trust in your brand.

## Step 9: Test hooks, not just ads

One of the best things about AI UGC is how cheap new versions are. Once you have a body that works, make three to five different hooks with the same creator and test them against each other. Our guide on [how to test video ad hooks](/blog/how-to-test-video-ad-hooks) shows how, even on a small budget.

## Signs your AI UGC ad looks fake

- **Skin that's too smooth** and lighting that's too perfect.
- **A cinematic blurred background** instead of a normal room.
- **Flat, slow delivery** with no energy or gestures.
- **Long pauses** because the dialogue is too short for the clip.
- **The face changing** between clips.
- **Random captions or music** the model added.
- **Claims a real person would never make**, like oddly specific results with no proof.

## FAQ

### Are AI UGC ads allowed on Facebook and TikTok?

Yes, AI-generated ads are allowed, but they have to follow each platform's ad policies. TikTok asks for realistic AI content to be labelled, and Meta labels some AI ads automatically. Don't use AI to make fake testimonials or misleading claims.

### Can an AI creator say they've used my product?

Not honestly, because they haven't. In the US, the FTC bans testimonials that claim to come from people who don't exist. Use the AI creator as a presenter or to share real customer reviews instead.

### How do I make AI UGC look less fake?

Use a real-looking room with natural daylight, ask for natural skin texture and small imperfections, avoid heavy background blur, give the creator lively delivery with natural gestures, and fill each clip with dialogue so there are no dead pauses.

### How long should an AI UGC ad be?

Most UGC ads run 15 to 45 seconds. A 30-second ad is about 70 to 80 words. Start there and cut anything that doesn't move the viewer toward the click.
