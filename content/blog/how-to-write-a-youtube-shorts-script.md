A good YouTube Shorts script isn't about clever writing. It's about never giving people a reason to swipe. You get about a second to earn the next five, and every line after that has to keep earning it.

Most Shorts lose people in the same three places. A slow first line. A middle that wanders. An ending that just stops. Fix those three and your watch time goes up, even with the same idea and the same camera.

Here's how to write each part, plus a template you can copy and a full example script.

## Why most Shorts lose people

Swiping is free. On a long video, people give you a minute because they chose to click. On Shorts, the next video is one thumb movement away, and it's already loading.

So people don't leave because your Short is bad. They leave because, for one second, it's less interesting than whatever comes next.

That changes how you write. You're not building up to a point. You're holding attention line by line, and each line has one job: make the next line worth hearing.

## Start with the payoff, not the setup

The first line is the whole game. If it's flat, nobody hears the good part.

A strong first line does one of three things. It names a result, a problem, or a surprise. And it does it straight away.

> Weak: "So today I want to talk about phone batteries."
>
> Strong: "Your phone battery hates being at 100%."

The weak one is setup. The strong one is the payoff, said first. People stay to find out why.

A few rules that work almost every time:

- Keep the first line under 12 words.
- Never open with "Hey guys", "Welcome back" or "In this video".
- Use a real number if you have one. "$22" beats "cheap".
- Show it in the first frame too. If you say "look at this", show it right then.

**If your first line could be cut without losing anything, cut it and start with the second one.**

## Pick one idea and cut everything else

A Short has room for one idea. Not three tips. Not a story plus a lesson plus a plug. One.

Here's a quick test. Can you say the point of your Short in one sentence? If you need "and" twice, you've got two Shorts.

Then check the length. People speak at about 2.5 words per second on camera, so:

- 15 seconds is about 35 words
- 30 seconds is about 75 words
- 60 seconds is about 150 words

Write it long first. Then cut about a third. The lines you cut are almost always the ones that explain what people already understood.

## Write for the ear, not the page

A Shorts script gets spoken, not read. That changes the rules.

Use short sentences. Most of yours should be 5 to 12 words. Use contractions, the way you actually talk. Say "you", not "people" or "viewers".

Read every line out loud before you film. If you trip on a line, so will your viewers. Rewrite it until it's easy to say.

Think of each line as one shot. When the line changes, the picture changes. A new angle, a quick zoom, a close-up of the thing you're talking about. Aim for a change every 2 to 3 seconds. It keeps eyes on the screen, even when the words are simple.

## Give people a reason to stay to the end

Once you've got them past the first line, you need a reason for them to stay. The easiest one is an open loop.

An open loop is a promise you make early and keep late.

> "There are three of these. The third one is the one nobody does."

Now people want the third one. They'll sit through the first two to get it. Just make sure the payoff is real, or they won't trust your next Short.

And don't end on "thanks for watching" or "follow for more". By then, the people who'd follow already have. Everyone else swipes on the dead line, and that drop-off shows up right at your ending.

## End on a loop

Shorts replay on their own when they finish. You can use that.

Write your last line so it leads straight back into your first line. When the video restarts, it sounds like one continuous thought. People often watch a second time before they notice.

> Last line: "And it all comes down to one simple fact."
>
> First line: "Airplane windows are round because square ones tore planes apart."

Played on repeat, those two lines join up. That's the loop.

## A simple YouTube Shorts script template

Here's a 30-second template. Fill in each line, then read it out loud and trim.

| Part | Time | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0 to 2s | The payoff, problem or surprise, in under 12 words |
| Setup | 2 to 6s | One line of context, only if people need it |
| Point | 6 to 20s | The one idea, in 3 to 5 short lines |
| Payoff | 20 to 26s | The answer, the result or the twist |
| Loop | 26 to 30s | A line that leads back into the hook |

And here's what that looks like filled in, at about 75 words:

> "Airplane windows are round because square ones tore planes apart.
>
> The first jet airliners had windows with sharp, squarish corners.
>
> Every time a plane climbs and lands, the cabin stretches and squeezes.
>
> All that stress piles up in the corners. Over time, the metal cracks right there.
>
> In 1954, two of those jets broke apart in the air.
>
> Round windows spread the stress evenly. Nothing builds up in one spot.
>
> That's why every plane window you've seen has curves. And it all comes down to one simple fact."

Every line is short. Every line is one picture. There's one idea, and the last line loops into the first.

## Common mistakes to skip

These are the ones that cost the most views:

- An intro before the hook. Logos, greetings and "quick one today" all come before the reason to stay.
- Too many ideas. If it's a list of seven, it's probably three separate Shorts.
- Lines written like a blog post. Long sentences sound fine in your head and terrible out loud.
- A weak payoff. If the hook promises a surprise, the answer has to be one.
- A dead ending. Finish on the loop, not on a request.

## FAQ

### How long should a YouTube Shorts script be?

About 75 words for 30 seconds and about 150 for a minute. Shorts can run up to 3 minutes, but most of the ones that hold attention are under 60 seconds.

### What's the best way to start a YouTube Short?

Say the payoff or the problem in the first line, and show it in the first frame. No intro, no greeting. Under 12 words is a good target.

### Do I need a script for YouTube Shorts?

You don't have to have one, but even five short lines stop you rambling. Shorts made from a script are usually tighter and easier to edit.

### How do I make a Short loop?

Write the last line so it leads straight into the first one. When the Short replays, it sounds like one thought, and people often watch twice.
